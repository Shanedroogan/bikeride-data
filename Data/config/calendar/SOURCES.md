# Holiday calendar, 2026–2027

`holidays.csv` is the single holiday source for the pipeline. The `config` artifact ships it
(`calendar.holidays`; the links builder reads it from there for the hops' one-seat reference
day), and the flows compiler and the validation gate read this file directly, so `flows` keeps an
empty `builtAgainst`. Nothing else keeps its own holiday list:
`lirr-off-peak.csv` beside it only flags some of these dates for the LIRR peak rule (every date
it lists must be here; `../SOURCES.md`).

## Format

`date,name,profile`, one row per holiday, with a header row.

- `date`: `YYYYMMDD`, the day that is off (see *Observed days*), parsed with
  `ServiceDate(yyyymmdd:)`.
- `name`: display name. No commas, so the file needs no CSV quoting.
- `profile`: `weekend` or `weekday`. This is the day type used for Citi Bike demand (the flows
  day type, and later the availability day type). `weekend` means "treat this date like a
  Saturday or Sunday". It is **not** the MTA service calendar: which schedule a transit system
  runs on a holiday comes from its GTFS `calendar_dates.txt`, never from here.

The validation gate's trip-count check does not read `profile`. It treats every listed date as a
holiday: excluded from its weekday medians and, when the previous build did not cover the date,
compared with the nearest of its weekday, Saturday and Sunday profiles (M1 audit, gate rule (a);
see `Data/gate/SOURCES.md`).

Invariants (readers may assert them): every date is a Monday–Friday, dates are unique and
strictly ascending, and `profile` is one of the two values above.

## Coverage

2026-01-01 through 2027-12-31: the 11 US federal holidays of 5 U.S.C. 6103(a) in each year, plus
the Friday after Thanksgiving (not a federal holiday; included because Citi Bike demand that day
is weekend-shaped). 25 rows. A date after 2027-12-31 is not known to be an ordinary day: extend
the file before any build window (trip months, timetable coverage) reaches 2028.

## Observed days

The federal rule for a fixed-date holiday that falls on a weekend (5 U.S.C. 6103(b), Executive
Order 11582; OPM's "Federal Holidays" tables): a Saturday holiday is observed the Friday before, a
Sunday holiday the Monday after. Only the observed (weekday) date is listed; the weekend date
itself is already a weekend day, and listing it with a `weekday` profile would wrongly turn a
Saturday into a weekday. In this range:

| Holiday | Falls on | Listed date |
|---|---|---|
| Independence Day 2026 | Sat 2026-07-04 | Fri 2026-07-03 |
| Juneteenth 2027 | Sat 2027-06-19 | Fri 2027-06-18 |
| Independence Day 2027 | Sun 2027-07-04 | Mon 2027-07-05 |
| Christmas Day 2027 | Sat 2027-12-25 | Fri 2027-12-24 |
| New Year's Day 2028 | Sat 2028-01-01 | Fri 2027-12-31 |

The last row is inside this file's range although the holiday belongs to 2028: OPM lists the
Friday before as the day off, as it did for 2021-12-31 (New Year's Day 2022).

The floating holidays follow their statutory rules: Martin Luther King Jr. Day and Washington's
Birthday are the third Monday of January and February, Memorial Day the last Monday of May,
Labor Day the first Monday of September, Columbus Day the second Monday of October and
Thanksgiving the fourth Thursday of November. The dates were generated by a script applying
these rules and checked by hand against the weekday of each date.

## Profiles

From the M1 audit (2026-09-26), pending a winter-month data check:

- `weekend`: New Year's Day, Memorial Day, Independence Day (observed), Labor Day, Thanksgiving,
  the Friday after Thanksgiving and Christmas Day (observed). July 3, 2026, the observed
  Independence Day, had a weekend-shaped Citi Bike profile in the July 2026 trip data (AM share
  0.118 against 0.14–0.25 on normal weekdays).
- `weekday`: Martin Luther King Jr. Day, Washington's Birthday, Juneteenth, Columbus Day and
  Veterans Day. Many employers stay open, so these are left as weekdays until the trip data for
  202511 and 202601 is checked.

Not yet decided: Dec 24 and Dec 31 when they are ordinary weekdays (not listed), and whether
Fri 2027-12-31 behaves like a weekend (listed as `weekend` under the New Year's rule; unchecked).
